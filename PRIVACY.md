# Shield by VPNInsights privacy policy

Last updated: 12 November 2026 (version 1.3)

Shield runs on your device. It has no account, no analytics, no advertising and no sale of data.

**What it stores.** Your settings, your per-site choices, and a count of blocked requests for the last 14 days. All of this stays in your browser's own storage on your device. It is never sent to us.

**What it sends.** Nothing, with three exceptions, each described below: the VPN tab, the password check if you turn it on, and a list web address you press Import on. The VPN tab: When you open the VPN tab, or press a button there, the extension asks vpninsights.com for your public IP address, place and network name, so it can show them to you. Nothing is stored on our side.

**The address-change warning (off by default).** If you turn on the VPN companion, the extension asks vpninsights.com for your public IP address every 5 minutes, so it can warn you if the address changes. That request reveals your IP address to us the way any website visit does. We do not log or store it. The companion is off by default.

**Weekly report.** The extension keeps, for the last 30 days and on your device only, a count of trackers blocked per day, the companies behind them and how many times you visited each site, so it can show your weekly report. It is never sent to us. You can clear it by removing the extension. A Monday notification shows last week's total; you can turn it off in Settings.

**Data breach warning.** The extension contains a list of websites that have had public data breaches (from Have I Been Pwned, CC BY 4.0). It compares the site you are on with that list on your device. The sites you visit are not sent anywhere.

**Fingerprint shield.** When on, a script changes what a page can read from your browser's canvas, audio and hardware settings. It runs on your device and sends nothing.

**Password check (off by default).** If you turn it on, when you finish typing a password the page turns it into a one-way hash on your device and the extension sends only the first 5 characters of that hash to the Have I Been Pwned service (api.pwnedpasswords.com), which answers with a list of many hashes that start the same way. The comparison happens on your device. Your password, and the rest of its hash, are never sent to us or to them.

**Links.** A small script replaces search-engine and social-network tracking redirects with the real address, on your device.

**Custom rules and lists.** Rules and lists you add are stored on your device. If you import a list from a web address, that address is fetched once, when you press Import.

**Cookie banners.** A small script runs on pages to find a consent pop-up and press its "reject" or "necessary only" button. It reads the page on your device. It never presses "accept" and sends nothing anywhere.

**Why it needs its permissions.**
- Read and change pages on all sites: to block trackers, remove tracking from links, add the Global Privacy Control signal and control scripts.
- Web requests: to count what was blocked and list it for you.
- Cookies and browsing data: to clear cookies when you close a site, if you turn that on.
- Privacy: to hide local network addresses from WebRTC, if you leave that on.
- Notifications: to warn you when your IP address changes, if you turn the companion on.
- Storage, alarms, tabs, scripting: to keep your settings, run the timers, know which site a tab shows and set the privacy flag in pages.

**Third parties.** None. The blocking list is bundled with the extension. We do not load remote code.

**Contact.** Questions go to the address on vpninsights.com.
